# High-Demand Data Gaps

Date: 2026-05-06

## What changed from round one

Round one leaned too much toward surface-level service concepts. Round two focuses on specific operational gaps where organizations already spend money and where the founder can win through research, data cleanup, packaging, and AI leverage.

## Research-backed gaps

### 1. Student housing operators have a signal problem

- Yardi Matrix reported softer rent growth and a more price-sensitive renter base in April 2026.
- TULU's April 2026 summary of Interface Student Housing highlighted a gap between what operators think students want and what students actually do.
- Studenthaus and BONARD both show that operators pay for market intelligence tools, proving there is budget for decision-support data.

**Implication:** There is room for a narrow campus-by-campus intelligence product or service that turns student behavior, competitor pricing, and leasing friction into action.

### 2. Small businesses need help applying AI to operations, especially pricing and margin control

- Goldman Sachs reported in March 2026 that 93% of small businesses using AI saw positive impact, but only 14% had fully integrated it and 73% wanted more training/resources.
- QuickBooks reported in February 2026 that the top operational challenge for SMBs was accurately predicting costs and profit margins when creating price estimates.
- Jobber reported that 20% of home service businesses struggle with pricing or margins, and ServiceTitan/Aspire research points to growing pressure on profitability, not just growth.

**Implication:** A data-heavy AI service around quoting, job costing, margin visibility, and follow-up has clearer ROI than generic AI consulting.

### 3. Real estate adoption is high, but the workflow is still fragmented

- Zillow's February 2026 agent trends survey said ease of use outranks cost and that a typical agent still uses two to four tools weekly.
- Delta Media's January 2026 survey found 97% of brokerages report agent AI use, which means the market is past “what is AI?” and into “make this actually useful.”

**Implication:** The opportunity is not another generic content tool. It is a “brainpower saver” that consolidates messy data into decision-ready outputs for agents and sellers.

### 4. Marketers and operators have more data than insight

- Brandwatch reported in March 2026 that only 25% of marketers say they understand their audiences very well.
- Sprout Social reported in April 2026 that 86% of organizations believe they missed opportunities due to delayed or siloed insights, and only 10% can act on real-time social data within hours.

**Implication:** There is a sellable gap for insight-as-a-service, especially if it leads directly to better pricing, positioning, or content decisions.

### 5. Private practices feel financial pain but rarely use AI for money decisions

- Heard's 2026 private practice report says therapists' top challenges are client acquisition and financial tasks; only 5% use AI for financial forecasting.
- MGMA's 2026 burden report says 95% of practices report growing regulatory burden.

**Implication:** The sharper wedge is financial clarity and admin visibility, not generic “AI assistant” positioning.

## Working principle

The best next ideas should be:

- data-heavy behind the scenes
- close to revenue or margin impact
- deliverable as a service first
- narrow enough to message simply
- expandable into software later
