# Decision Memo: Auburn Student Housing Intelligence Desk

## Verdict: TEST

**Score: 73/100** | Confidence: high

---

## Why This Score

This is a real opportunity, not a fantasy. The strongest part is the wedge: Auburn-specific renter intelligence for operators is much sharper than generic “student housing help,” and it fits your research-heavy strengths. The reason this is a **test** instead of **pursue** is simple: the market exists, but your ability to get paid for this exact local format is still unproven.

## Top 3 Strengths

1. **Founder-Market Fit** (88/100): You have Auburn access, finance/real-estate interest, research skill, and a local-student vantage point that enterprise data vendors do not.
2. **Demand** (84/100): In April 2026, Yardi/BD+C described student housing as more price-sensitive and fundamentals-driven, and TULU highlighted a gap between what operators assume students want and what they actually need.
3. **Monetization** (76/100): Studenthaus, Yardi Matrix, and BONARD all prove buyers pay for student-housing intelligence, and your offer can start with a paid pilot instead of needing software.

## Top 3 Risks

1. **Retention** (52/100): Operators may like a pilot but not renew monthly if the insights feel seasonal or duplicative. If you ignore this, you build a nice one-off service instead of a durable business.
2. **Competition** (61/100): Big intelligence vendors already exist and can claim “we do this already.” If you ignore this, you drift into a broad dashboard battle you cannot win.
3. **Distribution** (68/100): There is no viral loop and no bootstrap-friendly paid engine. If you ignore this, you end up with a smart offer that never gets enough at-bats in front of real buyers.

## Riskiest Assumption

The assumption most likely to kill this idea:

> "Auburn-area operators will pay me for a local intelligence brief instead of relying on existing reports, surveys, and internal judgment."

**Test it before building anything.** Build a list of 15 Auburn-area operators or leasing teams, create one polished sample brief, and offer a **$250 paid pilot**. Run this for 14 days at near-zero cost. A pass is **3 meetings and 1 paid pilot** from that list.

## Pre-mortem: If This Fails in 12 Months

1. Operators say the insight is interesting but not essential, so the service never becomes recurring.
2. Enterprise platforms or internal teams cover enough of the need, leaving only a weak “nice-to-have” gap.
3. The founder spends too much time producing custom research for too few buyers, and the business never standardizes.

---

## What To Do Now

Do **not** build software. Run the paid-pilot test first. In the next 7 days, make one sample Auburn renter-intelligence brief using public listings, amenity comparisons, student interviews, and review sentiment, then send it to 15 targeted local prospects.

**Kill criteria:** If you cannot get **1 paid pilot** after 15 targeted prospects and a strong sample brief, stop treating this as a top idea and pivot.

## If That Doesn't Work

Keep the same core skill set, but pivot the wedge to either **Quote & Margin AI Desk** or **Agent Brainpower Ops Desk**, where the ROI is easier to explain in dollars.
